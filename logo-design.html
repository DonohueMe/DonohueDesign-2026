Logo Design in Santa Rosa, CA

Your logo is the first handshake. Customers decide if they trust you before they've read a word — and if yours looks like every other business in Sonoma County, that decision isn't going your way. I build marks that earn attention on a truck, a storefront, a business card, and a phone screen. Same mark. Same power. Every size.

I've spent 30+ years in design — senior UX roles at Wells Fargo, Deloitte, Intel Security, and Apple, plus three decades running my own shop for clients across Marin, San Francisco, the East Bay, and the Peninsula. A logo isn't decoration. It's a working part of your business, and I treat it that way.

Signs yours is costing you money

Most owners can't name what's wrong. They just know something feels off. It's almost always the logo — and because your logo touches everything (website, cards, vehicle wraps, signage on Fourth Street, social profiles), one weak mark drags down every impression a customer forms.

The tells:

  • It looked great in 2008. A tired mark makes a healthy business look abandoned.
  • It falls apart small. If it blurs on a phone screen or social avatar, you're invisible in the places customers actually look.
  • You can't explain what it means. If it takes a sentence to defend, it's not working.
  • You get mistaken for a competitor. I've seen two Rincon Valley businesses running almost the same stock graphic.
  • You avoid putting it on things. That hesitation is your gut telling you the truth.

A weak logo doesn't just look bad — it costs you trust before the phone rings. Customers form a snap judgment in a fraction of a second. Your logo is doing heavy lifting whether it's earning its keep or not.

What you get

A mark built around your customer, not your taste. One that holds up on a billboard and a business card. One you're proud to hand out, stitch on a hat, and paint on a van.

You work with me directly. No account managers. No handoffs. No design team you'll never meet. Discovery, concepts, revisions, final files — all me, start to finish.

Every project includes:

  • Multiple custom concepts built around your business and audience
  • Final files formatted for web, print, signage, and social
  • A style guide so it stays consistent everywhere it lands
  • Direct collaboration with me from first call to final delivery

I also design websites, marketing materials, and AI tools that run on your own content. If you need more than a logo, it all comes from one studio — instead of three vendors who don't talk to each other and a brand that ends up pulling in three directions.

What to bring to our first call

Nothing polished. Just:

  • Three to five logos you like, and a sentence on why
  • Names of the competitors you're trying to stand apart from
  • Any existing brand pieces, even the ones you've outgrown
  • A short description of the customer you actually want walking through the door

That last one matters most. A logo for wine club members in Fountaingrove looks nothing like one built for a college crowd. I design for your customer, not your preferences — because your customer is the one writing the checks.

Don't overthink it. The first conversation is loose, and you're not locked into anything you say. That's the point of working one-on-one — a real conversation about what your business actually needs.
