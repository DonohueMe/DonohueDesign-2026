Logo design

Your logo shows up everywhere. Business cards. Invoices. Your website. Your truck door. It’s the first thing people notice, the last thing they remember. A good logo does real work for your brand.

I’ve spent 30+ years as a graphic designer, building brands for clients across Santa Rosa and the North Bay, including companies like Apple, Wells Fargo, Deloitte, and Intel Security. Now I’m based in Santa Rosa, bringing that same experience to businesses right here in Sonoma County. Every project still starts the same way it always has: a conversation about your business, your customers, and what this mark needs to do.

Most new clients come to me because they’ve outgrown a logo they made years ago, or they’re launching something new and want to get it right from day one. I design every logo custom — no templates, no clip art, no stock icon with your name typed under it.

Here’s what the logo design process covers:

  • Final files for print, web, and social media use

You work directly with me. No handoffs, no account managers. 30+ years of design experience, plus senior roles at Apple, Wells Fargo, Deloitte, and Intel Security, means I bring a strategic eye to every project. Your logo should earn its keep at every touchpoint.

Brochure design

A brochure earns its keep when someone has time to read — a tri-fold on your front counter, a welcome packet at a Railroad Square hotel, a stack at the California Welcome Center for visitors heading into Wine Country. It folds. It holds more. It sticks around long after a quick handout would be forgotten.

I design every brochure from scratch for Santa Rosa and Sonoma County businesses. No templates. Your piece starts with one question: what do you need the reader to understand before they call, book, or walk through your door? A real estate agent explaining a listing process needs room a single sheet can’t give. A dental office on Mendocino Avenue needs panels that build trust, not cram everything onto one page.

Here’s what brochure design typically covers:

  • Layout and visual hierarchy across folded panels — one clear path to your call to action
  • Copy editing and content flow so nothing feels cramped or cluttered
  • Print-ready files with correct bleed, fold lines, and CMYK color for your printer
  • Digital PDF versions for email attachments or your website download page

I’ve spent 30+ years designing print materials for clients across Santa Rosa and the North Bay. Downtown Santa Rosa businesses — tasting rooms near Fourth Street, professional offices around Old Courthouse Square, shops in Historic Railroad Square — rely on brochures that look polished enough to leave on a partner’s desk for months.

Every panel has to match your brand: colors, fonts, tone. A brochure that looks generic costs you the same trust a weak website does. Your brochure design page walks through my full process.

Flyer design

A flyer is built for speed — one sheet, one message, one action. Handed out at First Friday in the SOFA Arts District, posted on a community board in Art Alley, stacked near the register on Fourth Street before a weekend rush. Grand opening. Seasonal sale. Concert at Old Courthouse Square. Flyers work when you need volume, visibility, and a deadline that won’t wait.

That’s the part most people skip — a flyer that looks nice but buries the phone number isn’t doing its job. I design every flyer from scratch with one call to action front and center. No templates. No six competing headlines fighting for attention on a single page.

Here’s what flyer design typically covers:

  • Bold visual hierarchy — headline, offer, call to action, in that order
  • Copy tightened so every word earns its keep before someone walks past
  • Print-ready files sized for your run quantity, paper stock, and local print shop specs
  • Digital versions for email blast, social posts, or a QR code linking to your booking page

Santa Rosa businesses hand out a lot of paper — farmers markets in Historic Railroad Square, tasting rooms along Mendocino Avenue, storefronts between Santa Rosa Plaza and the McDonald District — and I’ve seen what lands on a counter and what ends up in the recycling bin. The difference is almost always structure, not decoration.

Need a brochure instead? Different format, different job. Your flyer design page walks through when a single sheet is the right call — and how I build one that actually drives foot traffic.

Email design for wineries and local businesses

Most emails from local businesses get deleted in seconds. The ones that get opened, read, and clicked share one thing in common: good design.

I build email designs that work hard for Santa Rosa businesses. Not drag-and-drop templates — custom layouts built around your brand, your offer, and your audience. Every email I design is built to render clean on phones first, since that’s where most people actually read them, often on a quick scroll between errands or in line somewhere.

Wineries along Westside Road and downtown tasting rooms have a real advantage here. Your customers already like you — they signed up for your list. But a plain-text email with a blurry logo doesn’t match the experience they had at your tasting bar. Your email design should feel like walking through your front door.

  • Seasonal release announcements that drive club signups
  • Event invitations with clear calls to action
  • Welcome sequences for new subscribers
  • Monthly newsletters that people actually open

A lot of business owners know they should be sending emails, they just don’t know how to make them look right. That’s the gap I fill. I’ve spent 30+ years designing for brands across Santa Rosa and the North Bay, and I handle the visual layout, the hierarchy, the responsive structure. You bring the message.

And here’s what matters most: an email that looks good builds trust at every touchpoint. It keeps you top of mind between visits, and that kind of consistency takes real design experience to pull off well on a small screen.

Presentation design

A bad slide deck can sink a great idea. I’ve seen it happen to business owners who know their stuff but lose the room because their slides are cluttered, hard to read, or just plain boring. Presentation design fixes that problem at the root.

Your content matters. The way it looks on screen matters too. I build presentations that guide your audience’s eye exactly where it needs to go — clean layouts, strong visuals, type that reads from the back of the room. Every slide earns its place.

Here’s what good presentation design actually does for you:

  • Keeps your audience focused on your message, not deciphering your slides
  • Makes complex data feel clear through smart visual hierarchy
  • Gives you confidence walking into any pitch or meeting
  • Creates a polished look that matches your brand across every touchpoint

I’ve built decks for clients pitching investors, presenting to city councils, and running sales meetings across Santa Rosa and the North Bay — including businesses right here at hotels near Railroad Square or offices out toward Montgomery Village. The stakes vary. The need doesn’t. You get one shot to hold a room’s attention.

Most people don’t think about presentation design until the night before a big meeting, then they’re scrambling. Better to get it right from the start. I’ll build you a custom deck that supports your story instead of fighting it. You work directly with me on every slide, no templates, no handoffs.

If you’ve got a pitch coming up, don’t wait.
